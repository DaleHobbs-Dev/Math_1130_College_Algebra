College Algebra — Course Objectives & Unit Outline (Math 1130)

This document summarizes unit topics, just-in-time remediation, and suggested applications to guide instruction and assessment across the term.

Exam 1 Material

Unit 1: Function Basics

Topics Covered
  • Relations and Functions: Determine whether a relation is a function identify the domain and range of a relation.
  • Function Evaluation: Evaluate functions for given inputs (polynomial, roots, absolute value).
  • Graph Characteristics: Use the vertical line test identify domain and range from a graph determine whether a point lies on a function interpret open vs. closed circles (optional: discuss continuity).
  • Just‑in‑Time Remediation — Exponents: Review product, quotient, power, and zero‑exponent rules.
  • Algebraic Operations on Functions: Perform addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of functions. Problems reinforce factoring (GCF) and FOIL if polynomial division is covered, express results in quotient–remainder form. Synthetic division may be previewed when graphing polynomials (covered more in Precalculus).
  • Function Composition: Find compositions and determine domains (and, when appropriate, ranges) of composite functions.

Unit 2: Exploring Linear Equations and Their Functions

Topics Covered
  • Solving Linear Equations: Include fractional coefficients recognize no‑solution and infinitely‑many‑solutions cases. (Optional fraction review assignment.)
  • Slope and Intercepts: Find slope and intercepts use slope with a point to write an equation (Point‑Slope Form included).
  • Graphing Linear Functions: Graph, and identify domain and range from graphs.
  • Parallel & Perpendicular Lines: Write equations through a given point parallel or perpendicular to a given line.
  • Applications: Solve real‑world problems using line graphs.
  • Systems of Linear Equations: Solve systems apply to real‑world, business, and mixture problems.

Unit 3: Factoring

Topics Covered
  • Greatest Common Factor (GCF): Identify and factor out the GCF. (Optional reinforcement.)
  • Factoring Techniques: Trinomials (leading coefficient 1 and &ne 1) factoring by grouping.
  • Special Products: Difference of squares perfect square trinomials sum and difference of cubes.

Exam 2 Material

Unit 4: Quadratic Equations and Their Functions

Topics Covered
  • Just‑in‑Time Remediation — Radicals: Simplify radicals to standard form understand basic radical constraints (e.g., no negative radicands for even roots) rationalize single‑term denominators.
  • Solving Quadratics: Solve using square‑root property, factoring, and quadratic formula (optional: completing the square, especially if conics are introduced).
  • Quadratic Functions: Identify vertex graph identify minimum/maximum, domain, and range apply to real‑world contexts.

Unit 5: The Big Family of Functions & Transformations

Topics Covered
  • Exploring & Graphing: Square, absolute value, cubic, and square‑root functions apply vertical/horizontal shifts and reflections across the x‑axis.
  • Equation Writing from Graphs: Write a function equation from a given graph. Vertical stretch/compression restricted to 1 or −1 (except parabolas, to reinforce quadratics and prepare for Precalculus).
  • Additional Concepts: Determine domain and range for all functions studied.

Unit 6: Rational & Radical Equations

Topics Covered
  • Rational Equations: Include chemistry‑relevant contexts require factoring where appropriate identify excluded values and invalid solutions discuss domain restrictions (without full rational function graphing). Many problems reduce to linear or quadratic equations to reinforce earlier skills.
  • Radical Equations: Solve and include examples with no real solution to build conceptual understanding.

Exam 3 Material

Unit 7: Inverse Functions

Topics Covered
  • Determine whether two functions are inverses.
  • Find inverse functions algebraically.
  • Identify domains and ranges of inverses and explain the domain–range swap relative to the original function (including rational and radical examples).

Unit 8: Exponential & Logarithmic Functions

Topics Covered
  • Exponential Functions: Explore graphs solve applications (including chemistry and biology) identify domain and range.
  • Logarithmic Functions: Convert between exponential and logarithmic forms evaluate common (log) and natural (ln) logs explore graphs identify domain and range apply to real‑world contexts.
  • Just‑in‑Time Remediation — Exponents: Review negative and fractional exponents to prepare for log properties.
  • Properties of Logarithms: Product, Quotient, Power Rules Change‑of‑Base Formula.
  • Solving Equations: Solve exponentials by rewriting with common bases (reinforcing exponent rules, especially the power rule) and using logarithms when appropriate.
