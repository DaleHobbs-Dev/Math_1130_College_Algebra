Course Description

College Algebra (3 Credit Hours) contains a study of expressions, equations, and functions of linear, quadratic, polynomial, rational, radical, exponential and logarithmic types. Applications of algebraic concepts will be emphasized throughout the course.

Exam Dates

TBR Charger Core Competencies

Critical Thinking

Analyze and evaluate ideas, issues, and evidence to form rational conclusions.

Scientific Inquiry & Mathematical Reasoning

Understand and explore the world through observation and experimentation, mathematical principles, and formal reasoning.

TBR (Charger Core) Category Learning Outcomes

  1. Apply mathematical principles or basic statistical reasoning to solve conceptual or applied problems and determine if solutions are reasonable.
  2. Communicate mathematical concepts using appropriate terminology and symbolic language.
  3. Analyze the behavior of, and solve, real-world problems relating mathematics to other disciplines.
  4. Use appropriate technology to analyze data and graphs, as well as model real-world behavior.

Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs)

  • Factor polynomials completely and solve polynomial equations.
  • Solve quadratic equations by factoring and applying the quadratic formula.
  • Simplify/perform operations on rational expressions and solve rational equations.
  • Simplify radical expressions and solve radical equations.
  • Determine if a given relation is a function.
  • Evaluate a given function using function notation.
  • Determine the domain and range of a given function.
  • Graph and analyze basic functions, including but not limited to linear, quadratic, polynomial, absolute value, square root, and rational.
  • Show that a function is one to one.
  • Find the inverse of a one-to-one function.
  • Convert a logarithmic equation to exponential form and vice-versa.
  • Simplify and evaluate logarithmic expressions.
  • Solve exponential and logarithmic equations.
  • Construct models using direct, inverse, and joint variation.
  • Solve real-world applications that are integrated throughout the course content.
  • Algebraically solve systems of linear equations in two variables (as time allows).

Methods of Instruction

What to expect

Weekly lectures with some lab days across the semester. To be successful, attend class, practice consistently, and use support resources (YouTube, Khan Academy, Tutoring Center, etc.). Keep up with MyLab Math assignments—don’t fall behind.

Student Responsibilities & Grading

  1. Attendance/Participation: Weekly homework objective checks (usually Sunday night / early Monday) verify at least 60% completion of the prior week’s assignments. About 13 checks at 1 point each. This category is 5% of your grade.
  2. Homework (MyLab Math): Assigned each lesson via MyLab Math All Assignments. Due dates are shown on the Online Campus front page (often the same day as the unit test). 30% of your grade. Late submissions are allowed with a 15% penalty on items completed past the deadline.
  3. Quizzes (MyLab Math): One chapter quiz most weeks (not on test weeks). Up to three attempts highest score counts. Access via MyLab Math Quizzes & Tests. Late work allowed with a 15% penalty after submission. Combined quizzes = 10%.
  4. Assessments: Three exams: Test 1, Test 2 (each 15%), and a cumulative Final (25%). Study guides and practice tests are provided in the content pages—use them!
  5. Drop Deadline: Last day to drop is November 1, 2024 (extenuating circumstances typically required).

Bonus Points

Earn up to 30 bonus points via campus activities, STEM Lecture quizzes, discussion forums, or tutoring participation. These may be applied to Test 1 or Test 2 (weighted at 25%). Bonus points cannot be applied to the Final Exam.

Academic Honesty Expectations

Academic honesty is essential to maintaining integrity in the classroom. Cheating and fabrication are forms of dishonesty that will not be tolerated. Such actions related to any academic exercise, assignment, evaluation, documentation, or assessment may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the entire course.

  • Fabrication: Falsifying or inventing information.
  • Cheating: Using unauthorized materials or aids, borrowing from others’ work, or allowing another to use your work on assignments meant to be completed independently.
  • Plagiarism: Submitting work as your own when it contains borrowed ideas, organization, wording, or other elements from another source without proper acknowledgment.

Working collaboratively with classmates or tutors is acceptable however, all assignments, projects, and assessments must represent your own understanding and self-expression. If you cannot explain or discuss the work you submitted, it may be considered plagiarized.

Consequences: Plagiarism or academic dishonesty may result in a failing grade on the assignment or the entire course, depending on the intent and severity (ranging from careless citation errors to deliberate copying). These offenses are serious and undermine your own learning.
