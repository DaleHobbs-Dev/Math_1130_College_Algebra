Solving Quadratics via Factoring

Why Factoring Helps Us Solve Equations

In this section, we begin talking about one of the applications of factoring. Not only is it really convenient to understand how to break polynomials up for the future sections we are covering, but we can also use factoring to solve some Blank equations (i.e. equations of the form $ax^2+bx+c=0$). To work this section, we will be using the Blank Theorem.

Zero-Factor Theorem: If $a$ and $b$ are real numbers and $ab=0$, then either Blank or Blank.

Steps to Solve Quadratic Equations by Factoring

  1. Set the equation to zero: Move all terms to one side of the equation so that the other side equals zero.
  2. Factor the quadratic expression: Factor the left-hand side of the equation into its factors.
  3. Apply the Zero Product Property: Set each factor equal to zero and solve for the variable.

Example

Solve the equation: $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$

Step 1: The equation is already set to zero.

Step 2: Factor the left-hand side: $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$

Step 3: Set each factor equal to zero and solve: \[ x - 2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 2 \] \[ x - 3 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 3 \]

Solution: The solutions to the equation are $x = 2$ and $x = 3$.
