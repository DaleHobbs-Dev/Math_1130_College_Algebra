Inverse Functions

Understanding Inverse Functions

Many real-life actions have reverses. For example:

  • Opening a window → the window
  • Picking up a book → the book and the room

Mathematical processes can also be reversed.

The inverse of “multiply by 2 then add 5” is:

by 5, then by 2.

Definition and Visual Meaning

Inverse Function

If \(f\) is a function, its inverse is \(f^{-1}\), where:

\(f(f^{-1}(x))=x\) and \(f^{-1}(f(x))=x\)

Properties of Inverses

  1. If \(f\) has domain \(D\) and range \(R\), what are the domain and range of \(f^{-1}\)?

  2. If \((a,b)\) is on \(f\), what point is on \(f^{-1}\)?

  3. Explain the difference between \(f^{-1}(x)\) and \(f(x)^{-1}\).

Steps to Find an Inverse

  1. Replace \(f(x)\) with \(y\)
  2. Swap \(x\) and \(y\)
  3. Solve for \(y\)
  4. Rewrite as \(f^{-1}(x)\)
