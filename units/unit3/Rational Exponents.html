Section 2: Rational Exponents

Definition

When you see the word “rational,” think of the word . In this section, we rewrite radicals as fractional exponents and vice versa.

\[ a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a} \quad \text{and} \quad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^m} \]

Example 1.

Rewrite using radical notation. Simplify if possible.

  1. \(x^{\frac{1}{2}}\)

  2. \(y^{\frac{1}{3}}\)

  3. \(16^{\frac{1}{2}}\)

  1. \(27^{\frac{1}{3}}\)

  2. \(x^{\frac{1}{4}}\)

  3. \(81^{\frac{1}{2}}\)

Now we extend this idea to exponents of the form \(\frac{m}{n}\).
