Graphing Quadratic Equations

What Does a Quadratic Graph Look Like?

Graphs of functions of the form \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\) take on the shape of a parabola.

Strategy for Graphing

Steps

  1. Find the vertex of the graph. The \(x\)-value is given by \[ x=\frac{-b}{2a}. \]
  2. Find the \(y\)-value of the vertex by plugging this value back into the original function.
  3. Use a T-chart to generate one or two additional points on one side of the vertex.
  4. Plot the vertex and your additional point(s), then reflect the points across the axis of symmetry to complete the graph.

Key Idea

Every parabola is symmetric about a vertical line called the axis of symmetry. This is why we only need to find points on one side and reflect them.

Example

For the following function, find:

  • How the graph opens
  • The vertex
  • Any \(x\)-intercepts or \(y\)-intercepts
  • The graph of the function

\[ f(x)=x^2-4x-5 \]
