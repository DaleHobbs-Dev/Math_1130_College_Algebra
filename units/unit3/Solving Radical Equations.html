Section 4: Solving Radical Equations

Steps for Solving Radical Equations

Step 1: Isolate the radical to one side of the equation (if possible).

Step 2: Square both sides.

Step 3: Finish solving.

Example 1.

Solve the following radical equations. Don’t forget to check your solutions.

  1. \(\sqrt{x}=4\)

  2. \(\sqrt{2x}=6\)

  3. \(\sqrt{x+1}=5\)

  4. \(4\sqrt{3x}=12\)

  1. \(\sqrt{5x}=-5\)

  2. \(\sqrt{2x-3}-2=1\)

  3. \(5\sqrt{3x+1}-7=13\)

  4. \(\sqrt[3]{4x}=-2\)

Example 2.

Solve the equation. This one requires isolating and squaring twice.

\(\sqrt{x+9}+\sqrt{x}=9\)
