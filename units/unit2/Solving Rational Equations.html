Solving Equations Containing Rational Expressions

Equation vs. Expression

Before starting this section, it is important to distinguish between an equation and an expression, because the main method from this section only applies to Equations.

Table: Equation vs. Expression Examples
Type What it means Examples
Equation Contains an equals sign and asks whether two expressions are equal. $2x+5=17$, $\cfrac{x}{3}-4=2$
Expression Does not contain an equals sign. It is something to simplify, factor, or evaluate. $2x+5$, $\cfrac{x^2-9}{x+3}$

Important note: expressions do not have an equals sign.

In this section, we want to get rid of all fractions before solving. This technique only works for Equations, not for simplifying expressions.

Solving Equations that Have Rational Expressions

Steps:

  1. Find the LCD for all denominators in the equation.
  2. Multiply every term on both sides of the equation by the LCD to eliminate All denominators.
  3. With the fractions gone, solve the resulting equation.
  4. Check all proposed solutions in the original equation. Any value that makes a zero appear in a denominator must be rejected as an extraneous solution.

Reminder: When solving rational equations, a value can appear to work after clearing denominators but still be invalid if it makes one of the original denominators equal to $0$.

Example 1.

Solve for the variable. Make sure to check your solution(s) in the original equation.

  1. $\cfrac{5x}{6}+\cfrac{1}{2}=\cfrac{x}{3}$

  2. $\cfrac{6}{x}-\cfrac{x+9}{5x}=\cfrac{2}{5}$

  3. $\cfrac{3x}{3x-1}+\cfrac{1}{x}=\cfrac{1}{3x-1}$

  4. $\cfrac{36}{x^2-9}+1=\cfrac{2x}{x+3}$

  5. $\cfrac{6}{x+3}=\cfrac{4}{x-3}$

  6. $\cfrac{3x}{3x^2+7x-6}-\cfrac{1}{x}=\cfrac{4}{3x^2-2x}$

Extra Practice

Solve the following. Answers are provided so you can check your work. Any extraneous solutions are noted.

  1. $\cfrac{x}{4}-\cfrac{21}{4x}=-1$

  2. $\cfrac{4x-1}{2x+3}=\cfrac{12x-25}{6x-2}$

  3. $\cfrac{7}{x-4}+\cfrac{3}{x}=-\cfrac{12}{x^2-4x}$

  4. $\cfrac{5}{n}+\cfrac{4}{6-3n}=\cfrac{2n}{6-3n}$

  5. $\cfrac{6}{w+3}+\cfrac{-7}{w-5}=\cfrac{-48}{w^2-2w-15}$
